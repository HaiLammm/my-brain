## Profile Summary

**Tomoko is the Japanese tourist who saved up for a 5-day Da Nang vacation and wants every moment to count. She did her homework on TikTok and Instagram — saw the Dragon Bridge, the Marble Mountains, and bahn mi that looks incredible — but now that she's here, she realizes none of that content told her HOW to actually do things. Which restaurant? How much should she pay? How does she tell the taxi driver where to go?**

Tomoko matters to DaNangNavi because she represents the high-volume traffic that makes the platform valuable to Vietnamese businesses. Every tourist who discovers great places through DaNangNavi and redeems a coupon proves to Lan the Listing Owner that the platform delivers real customers. Tomoko is the demand side of the marketplace — and her satisfaction drives the business model.

## Background

### Travel Profile

**Who She Is:** Office worker in Tokyo, 28 years old. This is her second international trip (first was Korea). She chose Da Nang because TikTok made it look amazing, it's affordable, and the flight is only 5 hours from Narita.

**Travel Style:** Semi-independent. Booked flights and hotel herself but hasn't planned daily itineraries. Prefers discovering things on the ground rather than rigid schedules. Travels with one friend who is equally inexperienced with Vietnam.

**Technical Comfort:** Heavy smartphone user. Instagram, TikTok, LINE, Google Maps. Used Tabelog religiously in Japan and expects similar functionality abroad. Will download an app immediately if it solves a problem.

**Language Situation:** Almost zero Vietnamese. Basic English for simple transactions ("how much?", "thank you"). Relies heavily on pointing, gestures, and translation apps. Deeply uncomfortable when communication fails.

---

## Current Situation

### The Tourist Reality

**The Daily Struggle:**
- TikTok showed beautiful places but never mentioned addresses, prices, or how to get there
- Google Maps reviews are mostly in Vietnamese or English — can't judge quality through Japanese eyes
- Taxi/Grab drivers don't understand her destination pronunciation — gets dropped at wrong places
- Menu prices seem to vary wildly — is 80,000 VND expensive or cheap for pho? She has no reference

**Skills & Tools:**
- Excellent at finding inspiration on social media — but can't convert inspiration to action
- Comfortable with apps — downloads and tries new tools quickly
- Good at reading reviews — but needs them in Japanese with Japanese taste preferences
- Budget-conscious — not cheap, but hates feeling cheated

**The Trust Gap:**
- Hotels recommend their own partner restaurants — feels like paid placement, not genuine advice
- Google "best restaurant Da Nang" gives SEO-optimized results, not authentic local picks
- Street food looks amazing but she doesn't know which stalls are safe for her stomach
- Wants to try local experiences but fears getting scammed or ending up in tourist traps

## Driving Forces

### ✅ Top 3 Positive Drivers (What Tomoko Wants)

**1. Maximize limited vacation time with efficient discovery of verified, quality experiences**
- She has 5 days. Every bad meal, wrong turn, or wasted afternoon is irreplaceable time lost
- Wants curated, reliable recommendations she can trust immediately — not another hour of Googling
- Success looks like: every day feels full of great experiences, zero "we should have gone somewhere else" moments
- **DaNangNavi Promise:** Senpai-verified listings with real photos and Japanese reviews, "Today's Best" curated daily picks, location-based discovery ("what's good within 10 minutes?"), category browsing by mood and cuisine

**2. Feel safe and confident despite language barrier through real-time communication support**
- The moment she can't communicate is the moment she feels vulnerable and helpless
- Wants to order food, negotiate prices, ask for directions — all without the panic of being misunderstood
- Success looks like: walking into any local restaurant, ordering exactly what she wants, paying a fair price — all feeling natural
- **DaNangNavi Promise:** Voice translation ("press and speak" Japanese → Vietnamese), visual menus with photos and Japanese descriptions, common phrasebook for tourist situations, emergency Vietnamese phrases with pronunciation guides

**3. Find exclusive deals and transparent pricing to avoid tourist markups and feel smart about spending**
- Japanese tourists are often targeted for higher prices — Tomoko knows this and it makes her anxious
- She doesn't want the cheapest option; she wants the FAIR option. Knowing she paid the right price is emotionally important
- Success looks like: using coupons confidently, knowing she got a good deal, feeling savvy rather than naive
- **DaNangNavi Promise:** Coupon system with clear discounts, "fair price indicator" on listings, senpai price reports ("I paid X for this"), deal-of-the-day features with genuine savings

---

### ❌ Top 3 Negative Drivers (What Tomoko Fears)

**1. Fear of being cheated on prices or led to tourist traps disguised as authentic experiences**
- She's heard stories of tourists paying 3x local price for the same pho. The thought makes her angry and embarrassed
- Tourist traps aren't just bad value — they feel like being disrespected, like being seen as a wallet, not a person
- Failure looks like: realizing after the fact that she overpaid, or that the "famous" restaurant was just famous for overcharging foreigners
- **DaNangNavi Answer:** Senpai-verified pricing, "tourist trap warning" flags on listings, community reports of price discrepancies, transparent dual-currency display (VND + JPY equivalent)

**2. Fear of wasting precious vacation days on bad recommendations from unreliable sources**
- A bad restaurant takes 2 hours (travel + waiting + eating). That's 2 hours she can't get back on a 5-day trip
- The compounding effect: one bad choice affects mood → affects next decision → can ruin an entire day
- Failure looks like: "We spent the whole afternoon at that place TikTok recommended and it was awful"
- **DaNangNavi Answer:** Only senpai-verified recommendations with recent reviews, honest ratings that include negatives, "what to skip" lists alongside "what to try", real-time community feedback

**3. Fear of embarrassment or helplessness when language barrier creates friction in public**
- Pointing and gesturing works for simple things, but fails for anything nuanced ("less spicy please", "can I get a different size?")
- Being unable to communicate makes her feel childlike and incompetent — the opposite of how she functions in Japan
- Failure looks like: giving up on a restaurant because she couldn't order, or accepting wrong food because she couldn't explain
- **DaNangNavi Answer:** Voice translation that works in real conversations, pre-set phrasebooks for restaurant/shopping/transport situations, cultural tips ("in Vietnam, you do X when you want Y"), empathetic design that normalizes the language barrier

## Validation Strategy

### What Tomoko Needs to See About DaNangNavi

**1. Instant Usefulness**
- First open → immediately useful. No signup wall, no tutorial
- "Near me now" results within 3 seconds
- Trust signals: "verified by 50+ senpai", "4.8/5 from Japanese visitors"

**2. Japanese-First Experience**
- Full Japanese UI — not translated-feeling, but native Japanese UX patterns
- Reviews from Japanese people with Japanese taste preferences
- Currency shown in both VND and JPY equivalent

**3. Deal Discovery**
- "Today's Deals" prominent on homepage
- Coupon works simply: show screen to cashier
- Clear savings displayed: "Save ¥500 (30% off)"

**4. Communication Safety Net**
- Translation tool accessible from any screen (persistent bottom bar)
- Works offline for common phrases
- Cultural tips context-aware: "At this type of restaurant, here's how ordering works"
