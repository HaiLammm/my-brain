# Lan the Listing Owner - Tertiary Persona

> TERTIARY target - Vietnamese business owner who funds the platform through paid services

**Priority:** TERTIARY 🏠
**Role in Flywheel:** Business owner who gains Japanese customers through DaNangNavi, proves the business model, and improves the Da Nang experience for all users
**Created:** 2026-04-07

---

## Profile Summary

**Lan runs a mid-range Vietnamese restaurant near the beach in Da Nang. She sees Japanese tourists walk past every day and knows they'd love her food — if only they knew she existed and could communicate what she offers. She's tried posting on Facebook and Zalo, but those platforms don't reach Japanese customers. She's heard that Japanese tourists spend generously and tip well, but she has no way to connect with them directly.**

Lan matters to DaNangNavi because she represents the revenue engine. When the platform delivers real Japanese customers to her restaurant, she's willing to pay for promoted listings and coupons. Her success proves the B2B model, attracts other businesses, and creates a virtuous cycle: more businesses → better deals for users → more users → more businesses. But she must see tangible results first — Vietnamese SMB owners are practical and won't pay for promises.

## Background

### Business Journey

**Company Role:** Owner-operator of a restaurant with 8-10 staff. Makes all decisions herself — from menu to marketing to accounting. Works 12+ hour days.

**Experience Level:** 7 years running this restaurant. Survived COVID, survived slow seasons. Knows her food is good — repeat Vietnamese customers prove it. But the Japanese tourist market is untapped.

**Technical Background:** Uses Facebook for Vietnamese customers, Zalo for messaging, basic smartphone skills. Has never used a business analytics dashboard. Her "marketing strategy" is word-of-mouth and a Facebook page updated irregularly.

**Management Style:** Hands-on, relationship-driven. Knows regular customers by name. Makes decisions based on gut feeling and peer advice, not data. Suspicious of complex digital tools — has been burned by "marketing agencies" that charged money and delivered nothing.

---

## Current Situation

### Business Context

**The Daily Reality:**
- Japanese tourists walk past her restaurant daily but don't come in — the menu is in Vietnamese only
- She tried Google Translate for a Japanese menu but the translation was laughably bad
- Competitors near the hotel district get Japanese customers simply by proximity, not quality
- She spends less than 30 minutes per day on marketing — the restaurant takes all her time

**Skills & Tools:**
- Expert at running a restaurant — food quality, staff management, customer service
- Basic Facebook posting — photos of dishes, occasional promotion
- Zero experience with Japanese language, culture, or marketing norms
- Learns from peers — "my friend tried X and it worked" is more persuasive than any sales pitch

**The Reach Gap:**
- Facebook targets Vietnamese customers — Japanese tourists don't use Facebook for restaurant discovery
- Google Maps listing exists but has few reviews and no Japanese content
- She can't create Japanese-language marketing materials — doesn't know what resonates with Japanese taste
- No way to track whether a Japanese customer came because of any specific channel

## Psychological Profile

### Personality & Motivations

**Core Identity:**
- **Pragmatic survivor** — didn't build a business by dreaming; needs to see results before investing
- **Relationship-oriented** — if she trusts someone, she's loyal and committed
- **Proud of her craft** — her food IS good and she knows it. The problem is visibility, not quality
- **Resource-conscious** — every 100,000 VND spent on marketing is money that could go to ingredients or staff

**Business Style:**
- Decides fast when the value is clear — doesn't need long presentations
- Needs social proof — "who else is using this?" matters more than features
- Prefers simple tools — if she can't figure it out in 10 minutes, she'll abandon it
- Measures success in customers and revenue, not impressions or clicks

## Driving Forces

### ✅ Top 3 Positive Drivers (What Lan Wants)

**1. Gain direct visibility to Japanese customers who are actively searching for her type of restaurant**
- She knows Japanese customers exist in Da Nang. She sees them every day. She just can't reach them
- Wants to be discovered by people who are ALREADY looking for Vietnamese food — not cold outreach
- Success looks like: Japanese customers coming in saying "I found you on DaNangNavi" — at least 5 new Japanese customers per week
- **DaNangNavi Promise:** Listed in Japanese-language restaurant directory with professional photos, searchable by cuisine type and location, visible to every Japanese person searching for food nearby

**2. Increase bookings and revenue without needing to understand Japanese language or cultural marketing norms**
- She can't write Japanese marketing copy. She can't design a Japanese-friendly menu. She shouldn't have to
- Wants a platform that handles the language/cultural bridge so she can focus on what she's great at: cooking
- Success looks like: Japanese customers arrive, order from a translated menu, pay, and come back — all without Lan needing to speak Japanese
- **DaNangNavi Promise:** Auto-translated listing in natural Japanese (not Google Translate quality), visual menu builder with photo upload, booking system that handles communication in both languages, coupon creation in 3 clicks

**3. Stand out from competitors through verified reviews and promoted placement**
- Her food is better than the tourist-trap restaurants near the hotels. But those places get all the foot traffic
- Wants a fair platform where quality wins — where senpai reviews reward good food, not just good location
- Success looks like: her restaurant rated higher than nearby competitors because senpai genuinely love her food
- **DaNangNavi Promise:** Senpai review system that rewards quality over proximity, promoted listing option to boost visibility, "Senpai Recommended" badge for consistently well-reviewed businesses

---

### ❌ Top 3 Negative Drivers (What Lan Fears)

**1. Fear of investing time and money in a platform that doesn't deliver real, countable customers**
- She's been burned before. A "marketing agency" charged 5 million VND/month and she couldn't tell if a single customer came from it
- Her trust threshold is high: she needs to see PROOF before committing budget
- Failure looks like: paying for 3 months of promoted listing and seeing no change in Japanese customer count
- **DaNangNavi Answer:** Free basic listing to start (zero risk), analytics dashboard showing views/clicks/bookings from Japanese users, coupon redemption tracking (physical proof of platform-driven customers), pay-for-results model aligned with her success

**2. Fear of complexity — managing an online presence in a language and platform she doesn't understand**
- She barely has time to update Facebook. A complex dashboard with analytics charts would overwhelm her
- If the onboarding takes more than 15 minutes, she'll quit and go back to what she knows
- Failure looks like: creating an account, getting confused by the interface, never logging in again
- **DaNangNavi Answer:** Vietnamese-language dashboard designed for non-technical users, onboarding with phone support in Vietnamese, "set it and forget it" option (basic listing requires minimal maintenance), WhatsApp/Zalo-based support for questions

**3. Fear of losing Japanese customers to competitors who get on the platform first**
- If her competitor down the street lists on DaNangNavi first and gets all the senpai reviews, she loses the first-mover advantage
- In a market this small (Japanese tourists in Da Nang), being second means being invisible
- Failure looks like: searching "Vietnamese restaurant near me" on DaNangNavi and seeing competitors but not her own restaurant
- **DaNangNavi Answer:** Proactive outreach to quality restaurants (not just whoever signs up first), category coverage ensuring fair representation, "new listing" boost to help latecomers catch up, quality-over-seniority ranking algorithm

## How Lan Discovers DaNangNavi Value

### The Recognition Path

**Phase 1: Experience the Difference**
- A Japanese customer shows her their phone: "I found you on DaNangNavi"
- She notices 2-3 more Japanese customers per week mentioning the platform
- A senpai review appears on her listing — detailed, genuine, positive

**Phase 2: Recognition**
- Realizes DaNangNavi is responsible for measurable increase in Japanese customers
- Sees her competitor also listed and getting reviews — competitive instinct kicks in
- Checks the business dashboard — sees actual numbers: views, clicks, bookings

**Phase 3: Investment**
- Signs up for promoted listing — wants to be at the top of search results
- Creates her first coupon: "10% off for DaNangNavi users"
- Uploads professional photos and updates her menu

**Phase 4: Advocacy**
- Tells other business owner friends: "You need to be on DaNangNavi"
- Becomes a case study: "Lan's restaurant got 30% more Japanese customers in 2 months"
- Provides feedback for platform improvement — feels like a partner, not just a customer
