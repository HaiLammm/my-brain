## Profile Summary

**Naoki is the Japanese professional who just landed in Da Nang — excited but overwhelmed, capable but disoriented. He's the kind of person who does things right: researches before deciding, asks the right questions, and pays attention to details. But in Da Nang, his usual methods fail. Google gives outdated info, TikTok gives vibes but no addresses, and his limited Vietnamese makes every transaction feel like a gamble.**

Naoki matters most to DaNangNavi because his journey IS the flywheel. When DaNangNavi helps him land on his feet, he develops genuine gratitude and expertise. Within months, he becomes the senpai who helps the next Naoki — creating the self-sustaining content engine that no competitor can replicate. Every satisfied Naoki is both a customer success story and a future content creator.

## Background

### Life Journey

**Career:** Software engineer at a mid-size Japanese company, transferred to Da Nang office. Alternatively: freelance developer who chose Da Nang for cost of living and lifestyle.

**Decision to Move:** Made the decision 2-3 months ago. Watched TikTok videos of Da Nang beaches, read a few Danang Holic articles, asked in a LINE group. Feels generally positive but realizes he knows almost nothing about actual daily life.

**Technical Comfort:** Very comfortable with apps and technology. Uses LINE, Google Maps, and various Japanese apps daily. Expects digital solutions to problems. Frustrated when information is only available through phone calls or in-person visits.

**Language Situation:** N5-level Vietnamese at best (knows "xin chao" and numbers). Conversational English. Relies heavily on Google Translate. Can read some Chinese characters (kanji) which occasionally helps with Vietnamese signage.

## Current Situation

### The First-Week Reality

**The Daily Struggle:**
- Arrived with a suitcase and a hotel booking — needs to find an apartment within days
- Every meal is a guessing game — can't read menus, doesn't know prices, fears being overcharged
- Commute to work involves navigating traffic that defies everything he learned in Japan
- Banking, SIM card, visa extension — each task requires navigating systems designed for Vietnamese speakers

**Skills & Tools:**
- Strong research ability — but all his usual sources (Japanese blogs, Tabelog, etc.) don't cover Da Nang
- Tech-savvy — can use any app, but needs to know WHICH app
- Self-reliant — prefers to solve problems independently, but language barrier forces dependence on others
- Learning attitude — willing to learn Vietnamese eventually, but needs to survive the first weeks NOW

**The Information Gap:**
- Danang Holic gives quality articles but can't answer his specific situation ("which neighborhood for my office location?")
- TikTok shows beautiful places but never mentions practical details (prices, how to get there, what to order)
- Google Maps reviews are in Vietnamese or English — rarely Japanese, rarely recent
- Asking colleagues helps but he doesn't want to seem helpless or burden them

## Psychological Profile

**Core Identity:**
- **Systematic thinker** — wants to understand the whole picture before acting
- **Quietly ambitious** — didn't come to Da Nang just for the paycheck; wants genuine cultural immersion
- **Pride in self-sufficiency** — being dependent on others for basic tasks is emotionally draining
- **Deep respect for local culture** — wants to do things "right," not just the easiest way

**Work Style:**

- Values efficiency — hates wasting time on things that could be solved with better information
- Community-minded — once he figures something out, his instinct is to document and share
- Perfectionist tendencies — won't recommend a restaurant unless he's genuinely confident about it

## Driving Forces

### ✅ Top 3 Positive Drivers (What Naoki Wants)

**1. Find trustworthy, step-by-step guidance for first-week essentials from people who've been through it**
- Naoki doesn't need generic travel tips — he needs a senpai who moved to Da Nang last year to say "here's exactly what I did, in this order, and here's what I wish I knew"
- This is about survival efficiency: housing, banking, SIM, transport, food — solved systematically, not through trial and error
- Success looks like: first week completed without major mistakes, feeling "I'm going to be okay here"
- **DaNangNavi Promise:** First 24-hour checklist, neighborhood guides by office location, verified housing listings with fair-price indicators, "Naoki's First Week" onboarding flow

**2. Build a social circle with other Japanese expats to combat isolation and create belonging**
- Moving abroad is exciting until the first lonely Sunday. Naoki craves connection with people who understand the uniquely Japanese experience of adjusting to Vietnam
- Not just networking — genuine community where he can ask "stupid questions" without judgment
- Success looks like: attending a weekend meetup within first month, having 3-4 friends who understand his situation
- **DaNangNavi Promise:** Community groups by interest/area, events calendar, senpai matching for newcomers, discussion forums where no question is too basic

**3. Feel confident navigating daily life without relying on Vietnamese language skills**
- Every transaction — ordering food, negotiating rent, going to the hospital — becomes anxiety-inducing when you can't communicate
- Naoki wants to feel competent, not helpless. He wants tools that bridge the gap while he learns
- Success looks like: walking into any restaurant and ordering confidently, handling a doctor visit independently
- **DaNangNavi Promise:** Voice translation for real-time conversations, visual menu guides with photos and Japanese descriptions, phrasebook for common situations, verified listings with Japanese-friendly indicators

---

### ❌ Top 3 Negative Drivers (What Naoki Fears)

**1. Fear of being isolated and overwhelmed in a city where nobody speaks Japanese**
- The worst scenario: sitting alone in an apartment, unable to solve basic problems, feeling like moving here was a mistake
- This fear is especially strong on weekends and evenings when work structure disappears
- Failure looks like: giving up after 3 months and returning to Japan, telling everyone "Vietnam wasn't for me"
- **DaNangNavi Answer:** Immediate community connection from day one, senpai welcome system, "you're not alone" messaging throughout the platform, active community that responds within hours

**2. Fear of making expensive mistakes due to language and cultural barriers**
- Signing a 12-month lease on a bad apartment because he couldn't read the contract. Paying triple for a SIM card because the shop saw a foreigner. Getting the wrong medical treatment because of miscommunication
- Each mistake costs money AND confidence — compounding the feeling of helplessness
- Failure looks like: hemorrhaging money on avoidable mistakes, feeling like a permanent outsider being taken advantage of
- **DaNangNavi Answer:** Price transparency with fair-price indicators, verified listings with senpai reviews, contract translation guidance, "warning" flags on common newcomer scams

**3. Fear of staying in a "Japanese bubble" and missing authentic Da Nang experiences**
- Naoki didn't move to Vietnam to eat at Japanese restaurants every day. He wants the real Da Nang — the street food, the local festivals, the hidden beaches
- But without guidance, he gravitates toward safe/familiar options, creating the exact bubble he wanted to avoid
- Failure looks like: living in Da Nang for 2 years but only knowing the tourist areas, never building real cross-cultural connections
- **DaNangNavi Answer:** Cultural discovery content curated by senpai who've gone deep, local experience guides beyond tourist zones, cross-cultural tips, Vietnamese language learning resources integrated into daily use
